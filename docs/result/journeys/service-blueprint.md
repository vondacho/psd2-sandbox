# Service blueprint — screens and read models

> **Status:** proposal generated by AI. No UX research was supplied (`Q-02`), so every screen and
> every piece of copy is a hypothesis for UX review (gate G1). The screen (`SCR-*`) and
> read-model (`RM-*`) identifiers are **defined here and only here**.

`SRC-ADR` names four PSU-facing surfaces and one owner for each:

- the **TPP** manages the **consent screen** in the web browser, where the PSU grants or revokes a given TPP's access;
- the **CIAM** (Transmit) owns the **login screen**, in the web browser;
- the **bank mobile app** presents the **OTP, QR-code or biometric screen** on the enrolled device, at the request of the CIAM;
- the same app presents the **consent confirmation panel** on that device, where the PSU confirms the items of the consent.

**Two things the ADR does not name, and this blueprint will not invent.**

1. **A payment has no confirmation panel.** The device journey ends in a *consent* confirmation
   panel. Nothing shows a payment's amount and payee, which dynamic linking requires
   (`C-09`, `Q-17`). Every PIS row below marks the surface as undecided.
2. **The PSU has no bank surface for withdrawing access.** The consent screen is the TPP's, so a
   withdrawal there is `terminatedByTpp`; `revokedByPsu` is defined as a revocation towards the
   ASPSP (`C-07`, `D-09`).

Three browser pages between the login screen and the return to the TPP — waiting, outcome and
"no longer available" — are **proposed, not stated** (`Q-18`). They are marked so.

## How to read it

A blueprint separates what the PSU sees (**frontstage**: screens) from what somebody needs in
order to decide (**read models**), and from who produces that information (**backstage**:
components and contexts).

A read model is the event-storming `readmodel` card: *the information an actor or a policy needs
to take one decision*. Each one below names its consumer and decision, its data and sources, its
owning component, and the consistency it needs.

Diagrams: [`authorisation-screen-flow.puml`](../system/uml/ux/authorisation-screen-flow.puml)
and [`wf-app-consent-confirm.puml`](../system/uml/ux/wf-app-consent-confirm.puml). Component
ownership: LikeC4 view `psuScreens`.

## 4. UX requirements proposed (hypotheses for UX review)

| ID | Proposed requirement | Why | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `UX-01` | What SCA binds is shown where the PSU confirms: the items of the consent on the confirmation panel, and — once `Q-17` is settled — the amount and payee for a payment | Dynamic linking (`INV-AUT-05`); the Detailed Consent model | Security review; usability sessions |
| `UX-02` | **Decline is as easy and as visible as confirm**, on the SCA screens and on the confirmation panel | The PSU's control (`OBJ-02`) | Usability sessions |
| `UX-03` | The TPP is named as the PSU would recognise it, and the bank's identity is unmistakable on the login screen and on every panel | `H-02`, §4.9, `Q-33`, `Q-47` | Usability sessions; phishing review |
| `UX-04` | Validity and frequency are stated in plain language on the confirmation panel | The consent fields are technical (§6.3.1.1) | Comprehension test |
| `UX-05` | Every dead end offers a way back to the TPP: expired, used, failed, declined, no enrolled device | `TPP-Nok-Redirect-URI` (§6.3.1.1) | `RM-OPS-SCA-FUNNEL` |
| `UX-06` | The PSU is told, in the journey, where a withdrawal takes effect — at the TPP, at the bank, or both | `H-05`, `C-07` | Usability sessions; `D-09` |
| `UX-07` | No bank marketing on the login screen or on any panel | Keep the decision focused | UX review |
| `UX-08` | The hand-over from browser to device is explicit: the waiting page names the device that was asked, shows the deadline, and updates itself when the device answers | Two surfaces, often two devices (`H-01`, `RSK-06`) | Funnel: login → confirmation drop-off |
| `UX-09` | A payment is never confirmed on a surface that does not show its amount and payee | Dynamic linking; `Q-17` | Security and compliance review |

## 5. Accessibility

The standard is not supplied (`Q-38`). The baseline applies to both surface families:

- screen-reader labels for amounts, IBANs and dates;
- dynamic type without truncating amounts, payees or TPP names;
- contrast and target sizes per the bank's standard;
- an alternative to biometrics, which the CIAM's OTP method already provides (`SRC-ADR`);
- no time pressure without warning: show the SCA window (`Q-24`) on the waiting page and on the device.
