# WS-01 — Walking-skeleton delivery pack

> **Status:** proposal generated by AI. Delivery and operations review §5–§9 (gate G5); product
> confirms the slice (`D-07`). The walking skeleton deliberately delivers little business value:
> its purpose is to prove the path, the integrations and the deployment before the solution grows.

## 1. Technical learning objective

Prove, end to end and in a real environment, the four hops this design cannot verify on paper —
all four of which come from the revised context rather than from the specification:

1. **A TPP request reaches the bank's resource model through Finologee**, with the TPP identified from its certificate and the vendor's integration contract as it actually is (`Q-03`, `RSK-01`).
2. **The bank can decide an account read against a consent record Finologee masters** — including what happens when that record changes (`D-01`, `RSK-07`).
3. **A PSU authenticated on the CIAM login screen can be asked to confirm on an enrolled device, and the result can be bound to what they were shown** (`INV-AUT-05`, `Q-15`, `Q-16`, `RSK-02`).
4. **A call to DCP can be made with a PingFederate token obtained from a Transmit token** (`D-02`, `RSK-03`).

If any of the four fails, the architecture in [`../system/README.md`](../system/README.md) changes
before the MVP is planned. That is the point of doing them first.

## 2. Minimal end-to-end scenario

One AIS thread and one PIS thread, each as thin as it can be while still crossing every boundary.

**AIS.** A sandbox AISP posts a consent on one account for balances and transactions
(`createConsent`). The bank starts the authorisation implicitly and returns `scaRedirect`. The PSU
follows it, authenticates on the CIAM login screen, confirms identity with an OTP on an enrolled
device, and confirms the items on the consent confirmation panel. The consent becomes `valid`. The
AISP reads `getConsentStatus`, then `getAccountList`, and the read is decided against the consent.
The AISP then calls `deleteConsent` and the next read is refused.

**PIS.** The same sandbox TPP, with the PISP role, posts a single SEPA credit transfer, the PSU
authenticates and confirms identity on the device, the payment is submitted to a DCP stub with a
PingFederate token, and the TPP reads `ACTC` from `getPaymentInitiationStatus`.

**What the PIS thread does not do:** show the PSU the amount and the payee. No such surface is
named in the supplied context (`Q-17`, `C-09`). The skeleton therefore proves the *mechanics* of a
payment authorisation and **must not be read as evidence that the payment journey is compliant**.
That is stated again in §9.

## 4. Components and interfaces

LikeC4 view `walkingSkeleton`. Components: `CMP-FINOLOGEE-GW`, `CMP-XS2A-ADAPTER`, `CMP-CONSENT`,
`CMP-AUTHORISATION`, `CMP-PAYMENT`, `CMP-ACCOUNT-INFO`, `CMP-CORE-ADAPTER`, `CMP-PSU-CHANNEL-API`,
`CMP-XS2A-STORE`, `CMP-TRANSMIT`, `CMP-PING`, `CMP-MOBILE-APP`.

Interfaces: `API-XS2A-PROFILE` (the `x-delivery: WS-01` operations) and `API-PSU-CHANNEL` (the
`WS-01` operations). `API-XS2A-EVENTS` is MVP scope.

Stubbed on purpose: **DCP** (`A-10`, `Q-05`) — the stub still requires a real PingFederate token,
because the token hop is one of the four things being proven.

## 5. Deployment path

One deployable for the gateway (`D-06`), one pipeline, one non-production environment reachable
from Finologee and from a real enrolled device. The platform is not named in the supplied context
(`Q-06`), so the pack asks for whatever the bank already runs rather than proposing one.

## 6. Contract-validation approach

- `API-XS2A-PROFILE` linted, and diffed against the Berlin Group's published file before any client is built (`W-API-02`, `Q-07`).
- Contract tests for the WS-01 operations, driven from the example maps.
- `API-PSU-CHANNEL` contract tests between the app, the CIAM and the gateway.
- A compatibility check in the pipeline on every change to either contract.

## 7. Automated test path

The `.examplemap` files are the discovery source; `tests/acceptance/features/*.feature` are
regenerated projections of them (`docs/ai/doctrines/examplemap-doctrine.md`). The skeleton wires
the generated features to step definitions against the running system, so that a change to an
example map shows up as a failing build rather than as a stale document.

## 9. Risks, dependencies and open questions

**Dependencies:** a Finologee sandbox and its integration contract (`Q-03`); a Transmit and
PingFederate configuration for the XS2A journey (`Q-04`, `Q-15`, `Q-16`); at least one enrolled
test device (`A-14`); an environment (`Q-06`).

**Risks carried into the skeleton:** `RSK-01`, `RSK-02`, `RSK-03`, `RSK-07`.

**Questions that must be answered before or during the skeleton:** `Q-03`, `Q-04`, `Q-09`, `Q-12`,
`Q-14`, `Q-15`, `Q-16`, `Q-18`, `Q-45`, and — for the PIS thread — `Q-17`.

**A standing caveat, repeated because it is easy to lose.** The PIS thread ends with a payment
authorised by a PSU who was never shown the amount or the payee. The skeleton proves the
mechanics; it does not make the journey compliant, and `RSK-08` stays open until `Q-17` is
answered and `STORY-PIS-PAYMENT-CONFIRM` is scheduled.

## 10. Completion evidence

WS-01 is done when `EVID-WS-E2E`, `EVID-WS-TRACE`, `EVID-WS-CONTRACT`, `EVID-WS-DEPLOY`,
`EVID-WS-TOKEN` and `EVID-WS-CONSENT-PUSH` all exist, and when `D-01`, `D-02` and `D-03` have been
taken by the people named in [`../system/README.md`](../system/README.md) §4 rather than assumed
by this document.
