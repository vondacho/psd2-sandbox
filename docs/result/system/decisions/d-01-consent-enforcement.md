# D-01 — Enforcing a consent the bank does not master

> **Status:** proposal generated by AI. The recommendation is not a decision. Decides:
> architecture board with compliance.

## What changed, and why this is not the old make-or-buy question

`SRC-ADR` states it plainly: **Finologee manages the consent state.** That settles the
make-or-buy question — it was bought — and opens a harder one that the ADR does not address.

`SRC-IG` does not know about Finologee. It holds the **ASPSP** answerable for:

- the `consentStatus` a TPP reads (§6.3.2, §14.15);
- refusing a read when the consent is not `valid` (§14.15, §14.11.1);
- refusing a read past `frequencyPerDay` with `ACCESS_EXCEEDED`, counted per account and per PSU (§6, §14.11.3);
- `validUntil` as **the bank granted it**, which may be shorter than requested (§6.3.1.1).

So the bank must answer "may this TPP read this account, now?" on every account call, for a record
somebody else owns. That is `C-08`, and the risk it creates is `RSK-07`.

## Options

### A — Call Finologee on every read

The gateway asks the vendor for the consent on each account call and decides from the answer.

- **For:** one copy of the truth; a revocation bites on the next read by construction; nothing to reconcile.
- **Against:** the vendor is on the hot path of every read, so its latency and availability become the bank's. `frequencyPerDay` still has to be counted somewhere, and §6 says per account and per PSU — the vendor may not count it the way the bank must report it.

### B — Projection, kept current by vendor-pushed changes, with a staleness bound

The gateway holds a projection of the consent record (`A-16`). Finologee pushes creations,
authorisations, revocations and expiries. `INV-CNS-06` refuses a read when the projection is older
than an agreed bound, and a synchronous call to the vendor is the fallback on that path.

- **For:** reads are decided inside the bank at bank latency; `ACCESS_EXCEEDED` is counted where the invariant lives (`INV-ACC-03`); the failure mode is **refusing access**, not serving revoked data.
- **Against:** two copies, so reconciliation and drift monitoring are real work; it depends entirely on the vendor actually pushing revocations, which nobody has confirmed (`Q-45`).

### C — Projection refreshed by polling

Same as B without the push.

- **For:** needs nothing from the vendor beyond a read API.
- **Against:** the poll interval **is** the revocation delay. A PSU who withdraws access and sees the TPP still reading five minutes later is the failure `H-03` predicts, and the bank cannot explain it away.

## Recommendation

**B**, conditional on `V1`.

The deciding criterion is not latency, and it is not tidiness. It is this: when the bank cannot
vouch for its own answer, it must refuse rather than serve. `INV-CNS-06` is written to fail closed,
and B is the only option where that invariant is enforceable inside the bank at read latency.

A is the honest fallback if `V1` fails: it is slower and couples the bank to the vendor's
availability, but it is never wrong. C is not recommended at any poll interval, because the
interval is a promise to the PSU that nobody has agreed to make.

## What must be verified before the walking skeleton is called done

| | Verification | Why it decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `V1` | Finologee publishes consent revocation and expiry as something the bank can subscribe to, with a stated delivery guarantee (`Q-45`) | Without it, B degrades to C |
| `V2` | The bank can export the consent audit trail it owes supervisors, from a record it does not hold (`Q-45`) | `SRC-ADR` names auditing as the bank's responsibility |
| `V3` | The synchronous fallback's latency is acceptable on the read path | It is what B falls back to whenever the projection is stale |
| `V4` | Whether the vendor or the bank allocates the `consentId`, and whether `validUntil` shortening happens at the bank or at the vendor (`INV-CNS-05`) | It decides who the TPP is really talking to |

## Consequences either way

- The context map edge `TPP Access -> Consent` stays **conformist** while the vendor masters the record. If the bank ever takes the record back, that edge becomes customer-supplier and the `.ddd` must change with it.
- `RM-ACCESS-DECISION` is the read model this decision is really about; its consistency column in the service blueprint is the single line that changes between the options.
- `D-09` is adjacent but separate: it asks where a PSU withdraws access, not how the withdrawal is enforced once it exists.
